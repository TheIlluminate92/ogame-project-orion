QUICK START

How to run an Orion mission

The exact fleet and cargo depend on the mission card, but the operating flow stays consistent.

  1. 01
    Produce Lithium

    Build IAS levels, choose a source resource, and keep enough Lithium available for scanning and reward claims.

  2. 02
    Scan and choose quickly

    Select an anomaly level you can afford and review the mission type, PvE/PvP tag, waves, duration, distance, and possible rewards. Spawned missions are public and first come, first served.

  3. 03
    Send the right fleet

    Combat missions need enough fighting power for every wave. Delivery and transport missions need the required cargo and ships shown by the mission.

  4. 04
    Use ACS when offered

    Add your eligible fleets or invite alliance members and buddies. Keep a fleet slot free; you cannot start or join ACS when all slots are occupied. Rewards split by each participant's contributed fleet resource value.

  5. 05
    Watch the waves

    Hold and defend the position until each wave resolves. PvP missions pay more, but other players can attack the mission fleet.

  6. 06
    Claim the rewards

    Claim completed waves as you go or wait until the mission ends. If Lithium is short, claim a partial reward. A friendly NPC transport delivers it to the discovery planet and is visible on phalanx; unclaimed rewards expire one hour after the final wave.

PVE

Protected mission fleet

Other players cannot attack the mission fleet. Use this for lower-risk testing and learning the wave flow.

PVP

Double rewards, open risk

PvP missions pay twice the rewards, but the fleet can be attacked and recycled by other players.

ACS

Bring help

When ACS is supported and enabled in the universe, coordinate your own fleets with alliance members or buddies to hold the position. Keep one fleet slot available to start or join the operation. Alliance Depot levels across your planets increase the group member cap by one per 20 levels, up to five increases.

!Choose a manageable anomaly level

Higher levels can improve rewards, but they also increase combat difficulty plus scan and claim costs. The official FAQ recommends lowering the selected level when Lithium is tight.

    FIELD NOTES

    What to compare when choosing rewards

    Early PTS samples suggest useful questions to ask. These are working observations, not guarantees about every mission.

    iUse the reward and cost together
    • Compare resource rewards with the Lithium needed to claim them. The shared 3:2:1 Metal:Crystal:Deuterium benchmark helps compare mixed rewards. For players with surplus Food after population growth, Food may have little alternative use, so Lithium made from it can have a much lower marginal resource cost; the initial investment still counts.
    • A large payout can still be a poor return after claim costs. Include scan costs, time, fleet losses, and any player attacks when deciding what was worthwhile.
    • Delivery missions have often compared well in the current resource sample, while combat results vary. Mission type alone does not guarantee a good return.
    • PvP missions offer higher potential rewards and can be attacked by other players. Consider the risk to the fleet alongside the displayed payout.
    • Stars, anomaly level, wave count, and mission Distance have not been reliable standalone predictors of return in the samples so far. Scan reach and mission Distance are separate measurements.
    • Displayed possible resource rewards have matched collected amounts in completed tests so far. This is an observation to keep checking as more missions are claimed.
