The expedition path: build an operation you can repeat.
Skie Field Manual · Conditional Skie strategy based on the October 2026 administrator expedition guide; class and patch modifiers must match your universe

You will learn:
- Build a reliable expedition cycle before investing in its maximum theoretical output.
- Choose Lifeform upgrades by the bottleneck they remove.
- Track spendable resources, ship finds, fuel and losses separately.

Count the launches you will really make

This path rewards regular attention. Discoverer is the class to compare first for an expedition-centered account, but use the current class bonuses and your universe’s actual rules. Start with the hours you can relaunch, not the hours a spreadsheet assumes you are awake. Record a normal week including workdays and weekends. Mines remain valuable income while you cannot launch.

Do not fund the plan with a lucky screenshot
Resources and useful ship finds vary. Black holes, hostile encounters, empty results and delays are part of the operation. Keep a replacement reserve before expanding it.

Stage 1: build the working fleet and schedule

An expedition build has two different limits: the reward the mechanics allow and the cargo you can bring home. More cargo can solve a transport limit, but excess cargo does not endlessly raise the reward cap. Likewise, travel-speed improvements help only when shorter travel becomes an extra real cycle or a more reliable schedule.

1. Read the expedition mechanics guide. Confirm Astrophysics, total fleet slots, class, economy speed, relevant bonuses and the current reward bracket before calculating a fleet.

2. Create one replaceable expedition fleet with sufficient cargo and the appropriate support ships. Keep your core transport and fleetsave capacity separate.

3. Run a test cycle. Inspect the report and actual returning cargo. Check whether your normal launch time leads to a return you can cover.

4. Repeat only across the slots you can maintain. Save the last batch’s landing window with extra margin for delays before your planned offline time.

Stage 2: grow the bottleneck, not everything at once

If reports show insufficient carrying capacity, correct cargo before buying another reward bonus. If fleets sit at home after returning, a speed upgrade may do little for daily launches. If you cannot replace a lost working fleet, build the reserve before increasing launch size. If fuel interrupts the routine, fix Deuterium logistics rather than measuring success only in gross loot.

Once the operation is stable, compare slot-unlocking research, cargo capacity and reward bonuses. Price the entire change, including any extra fleet required for a new slot. Astrophysics that adds capacity is not free income if you cannot afford or relaunch the new fleet.

Keep a daily record of launches, resource returns, usable ship finds, fuel and losses. Treat findable ships separately from resources until you have a real use or conversion plan. A resource-weighting method must match your objective; a large Metal-equivalent total can still leave you short of Crystal for research.

Stage 3: choose Lifeforms around the operation

Compare the building benefits of Humans, Mecha and Rock’tal against your actual split of expedition and mining income. Do not confuse a planet’s chosen species with the technologies selected into its research slots. Once species are discovered, you can assemble research choices across species while keeping the buildings that fit that planet.

Review Enhanced Sensor Technology, Sixth Sense and Telekinetic Tractor Beam for expedition-focused technology choices. Discoverer Enhancement is relevant when you are actually playing Discoverer. Use the current descriptions and compare the next level’s benefit with the cost to unlock and develop it. The Lifeforms guide explains the population and artefact work needed first.

Cargo or speed selections can be sensible logistics choices, but they displace another technology in the same slot. Estimate the production you give up and ask whether the change increases useful capacity, actual cycles or quality of life. A percentage-point change in an additive bonus is not the same percentage change in your final account income.

Rebalance when your routine changes

Review several days or a meaningful batch, not one exceptional result. Change one variable at a time. If work or life reduces your launches, shift investment toward passive production rather than maintaining an old high-attention plan out of habit. The best expedition account is one that you can keep operating without sacrificing fleet safety or sleep.

A useful cargo decision
If a fleet can already carry its permitted maximum find, another cargo upgrade may reduce the number of cargo ships required per launch. That can lower replacement exposure or simplify logistics. It does not by itself prove higher rewards. Measure the benefit you are actually buying.

Before moving on:
- My fleet calculation uses the current account and universe inputs.
- I retain transport capacity, fuel and a replacement reserve outside expedition fleets.
- My next cargo, speed or LF upgrade solves a real measured limit.
- I record net returns across a batch and separate ships from spendable resources.
